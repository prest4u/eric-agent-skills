# Formal Teaching Contract

Load this reference only for a formal lesson, multi-lesson unit or course, capacity design, or differentiated learning paths.

## Formal lesson spine

Keep one bottleneck and one observable signal across this sequence:

1. **Diagnosis** — use a short answerable task to expose the likely bottleneck.
2. **Focused teaching** — teach one mechanism tied to the observed response.
3. **Guided practice** — scaffold the same mechanism and check immediately.
4. **Independent output** — remove the teaching scaffold and require student work.
5. **Feedback and retry** — name the observable error, repair it, and retry.
6. **Next task** — set one bounded retrieval or transfer task with a success signal.

If entry evidence is missing, put the diagnosis inside the lesson and state which branch follows each result. Do not demand a separate intake round when this can be done safely.

## Capacity

Assign minutes to concrete teacher and student actions until the total matches the available time. Protect diagnosis, independent output, and feedback; cut extra examples, extra rounds, decorative activities, and optional transfer in that order. State when the requested mandatory scope cannot fit and offer the smallest runnable version.

Use these profiles as heuristics, not fixed templates:

| Available time | Runnable capacity |
|---|---|
| 15 minutes | One item per stage and one immediate retry. |
| 30–50 minutes | One complete cycle, a small practice set, and one transfer. |
| 90 minutes | One bottleneck, two spaced practice cycles or a justified reset, then independent output and feedback. |
| 120 minutes | Phased cycles, a break or reset, and at least one transfer assessment; avoid repeated explanation as filler. |

For a 45-minute reading lesson with unknown level, begin with a short passage and two contrasting items, such as explicit evidence location and controlled inference. Branch the main practice from the observed result and still provide a complete runnable plan in the same response.

## Units, courses, and differentiation

Give each lesson a distinct purpose, capacity, observable check, source status, and dependency. Use the opening lesson for embedded entry diagnosis and show how later lessons adapt; do not repeat one template under different titles.

Keep a common goal and comparable final signal across differentiated paths. Change the entry task, scaffolding, practice path, feedback/repair, and next task—not merely the number or nominal difficulty of questions.

- **Foundation:** models and cues, reduced decision load, near transfer, immediate modeled retry.
- **Core:** standard entry item, conditional prompts after error, mixed guided and independent transfer.
- **Advanced:** ambiguity or transfer entry, criteria instead of worked answers, self-diagnosis and novel transfer.
