# Teaching Review Lens

Use this for lesson plans, worksheets, practice packs, feedback, teacher guides, student handouts, and K12 English materials.

## Required Checks

- Student fit: grade, current level, exam target, weak module, motivation, and prior context are explicit or honestly marked unknown.
- Scoring bottleneck: the material targets a real score-limiting skill, not a generic topic.
- Lesson spine: diagnosis, focused teaching, guided practice, independent output, feedback, and next task are coherent.
- Classroom capacity: timing fits the lesson length; teacher talk does not crowd out student work.
- Practice quality: tasks are specific, answerable, and tied to the stated objective.
- Source authenticity: official exam claims have source path, year, page/question number, or are marked unverified.
- Reuse risk: if the student/project context matters, check prior lesson directories before claiming material is fresh.
- Student-facing hygiene: no internal teaching language in visible materials.
- Student/teacher boundary: student materials contain no answers or teacher process. Unless authoritative requirements explicitly choose appendix-only mode, the teacher edition must reproduce every student task in the same order and place the answer/reference, task-specific evidence or rationale, needed explanation, common errors or wrong-option analysis, and repair/teaching note beside it or through an unambiguous nearby link. Build an internal task-by-task parity crosswalk; selected examples or distant answer lists do not prove completeness.
- Learning sequence: multi-unit books follow a defensible learner order. If the visible sequence merely mirrors source/production IDs, flag it unless the project explicitly approved that order.
- Leveled-course authenticity: for A/B/C, foundation/core/advanced, or similar tiered courses, verify the student bottleneck, lesson spine, practice design, and teacher guide actually differ by learner need. A lower tier that merely deletes B-track difficulty, or an upper tier that merely adds timing labels, is not a valid differentiated course even if all PDFs, manifests, and text scans pass.
- Foundation-track classroom capacity: a low-foundation lesson may use small steps, but it still needs enough teachable volume for the intended lesson length. Look for rescue drills, core repeat sets, a second independent item, extension sets, and teacher cut-time choices; a single micro-task plus one extension sentence is a sample, not a 120-minute lesson pack.
- Foundation-track bridge quality: when a lesson moves from line-bank or micro-text practice into a full passage or full writing prompt, the student page must include visible bridge scaffolds such as line numbers, sentence chunks, evidence bands, or prompt decomposition. Do not accept "teacher can give line numbers" as the only scaffold.
- Advanced-track exam surface: high-score or exam-simulation materials must not display generator placeholders such as `(blank)` in question stems; use real printed blanks or natural exam wording.

## Internal Language Leakage

Flag student-facing use of terms such as `挖坑`, `心法`, `出招`, `拆招`, `定招`, `教师动作`, `话术`, `不讲解只对照`, `观察真实`, `内部打法`, or source IDs. Recommend student-safe wording.

## Severity Calibration

- P0: wrong source claim, severe content mismatch, leaked internal strategy in public/student material, impossible classroom timing.
- P1: unclear bottleneck, passive lesson structure, insufficient practice, weak homework/next loop.
- P1: tiered-course materials where the level identity is mostly title/metadata/page-count changes rather than a changed teaching path.
- P1: foundation-track samples that are pedagogically correct but too thin for the intended classroom duration.
- P1/P2: advanced-track question stems with fake placeholder text, depending on frequency and centrality.
- P2: better sequencing, clearer instructions, stronger examples, improved personalization.
- P3: wording polish.

## Evidence Requirements

Cite section headings, page numbers, file paths, quoted student-facing text, timing totals, source references, and prior-material checks when available.

## Reviewer Non-Scope

Do not judge code implementation details or rendered visual defects except where they affect classroom use; route those to code or visual QA.
