# Lightweight Video Pre-production

Read this reference only when the current task needs a plan, script, storyboard, shot list, visual direction, or render-ready brief. Produce the least formal artifact that lets the next requested stage proceed.

## Choose the planning depth

- For an idea or early script, create a usable outline, script, or short storyboard. Do not force a full production spec.
- For a costly visual uncertainty, create one hero frame or short scene proof before scaling.
- Create `video-spec.md` only when the user requests it or implementation would otherwise require repeated creative guessing.
- When revising an existing plan, read it completely and preserve unrelated scenes and notes. Confirm a broad replacement or write a non-destructive sibling version.

## Lock only decisions that change the cut

Capture what is known and ask only for a missing choice that materially changes the result:

- one purpose and one concrete viewer
- primary platform, duration, aspect ratio, frame rate, and language
- one-sentence promise, opening hook, narrative turn, and payoff
- viewer knowledge boundary and desired action
- narration, subtitle, and audio intent
- visible visual system: palette, typography, layout, transition family, motion vocabulary, and anti-references
- assets and their status: existing, generated, placeholder, missing, rights-unclear, or private

Translate vague adjectives into two or three visible options with costs. For example, replace “premium” with concrete alternatives such as editorial restraint, cinematic dark-field storytelling, or a precise data-led grid. Recommend one when the evidence supports it.

## Scene contract

For each planned scene, include only fields the next stage needs:

- exact start and end time
- narrative role and viewer-facing payload
- narration and on-screen text
- visible composition and motion verbs
- audio or silence intent
- transition in and out
- asset dependencies, rights state, and fallback
- expected hero frame or QA proof point

Never invent a renderer component. Name a verified project component with its source, describe a generic feasible pattern, or mark a missing element with an owner/action and renderable fallback.

## Source-video references

Use `$eric-reach` for a public video URL or requested subtitles/visual analysis. Keep retrieved facts, captions, and visual observations distinct from inference. Reuse transferable structure—hook mechanics, evidence order, pacing, subtitle emphasis, and teaching sequence—while rewriting the argument, examples, visuals, and wording. Do not reproduce long transcript passages or imply rights to source media.

## Render-ready minimum

Call a brief render-ready only when it states:

- exact duration, dimensions/aspect ratio, and frame rate
- what the viewer sees and hears in every scene
- all on-screen text and subtitle behavior
- one coherent visual and motion system
- asset availability plus fallbacks
- what sampled frames and playback should prove

Mark unresolved choices plainly. A partial but honest brief is better than fabricated completeness.
