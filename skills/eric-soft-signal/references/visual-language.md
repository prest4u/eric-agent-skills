# Soft Signal visual language

Use warm paper, restrained contrast, and generous breathing room. Treat color as a learning signal: clay marks an active section or action, violet marks a strategy or evidence cue, pale yellow marks a caution, and muted ink carries supporting information. Keep the body quiet enough for handwriting and print.

## Compose the page

- Establish one primary action per section; give it the strongest heading and the clearest entry point.
- Interpret “one primary action per section” semantically, not as “one thin section per page.” A rule, its examples, a caution, and the immediately related practice may share one continuous page.
- Pair Chinese labels with concise English task language. Let Chinese orient the learner and let English carry the practice action.
- Use rules, spacing, and alignment to separate task families. Prefer an open line or compact table over dense decorative containers.
- Reserve writing space before finalizing text. Use one `soft-reflection` block for a reflection marker, heading, prompt, and ruled response area so these elements remain attached and cannot collide.
- Keep the cover or opening zone simple: title, learner purpose, and only the metadata needed for use.

## Choose the page mode

Use **compact handout mode** for a short worksheet, recap, or single classroom task. A deliberate page break and generous response space are appropriate when the student must write, mark, or speak from the page.

Use **editorial self-study mode** for a multi-part lesson, review book, or course booklet:

- Let content flow continuously. A new chapter or subsection may begin midway down a page after the previous practice concludes.
- Build a stable hierarchy: quiet running header; numbered chapter title; numbered subsection; core rule; example or contrast; caution or strategy; nearby practice.
- Keep ordinary knowledge pages visibly occupied—roughly 55–80% of the usable page height is a useful diagnostic, not a quota. Reserve very large blank areas for actual writing, reading records, chapter transitions, or intentional reflection.
- Avoid forced page-count padding and avoid one fixed module per page. Rebalance related content before shrinking type or creating a nearly empty overflow page.
- Use short page headers. Do not repeat the same lesson number, English label, Chinese title, and audience label in both the header and body.

## Use flat teaching areas

- Use a pale clay full-width area for a core rule, warm white with a fine border for examples or analysis, pale yellow for a caution, and pale violet for a strategy or teacher note.
- These are pedagogical bands or page areas, not a card grid. Keep corners quiet, avoid shadows, and do not wrap every small instruction or option in its own container.
- Use tables only for genuine comparison, classification, or recording. Alternate tables with prose examples, contrasts, short exercises, and open writing space so page roles remain distinct.
- Keep teacher guidance adjacent to the matching student task at a readable body size. Do not compress a complete teacher explanation into a tiny footer block.
- Do not copy every student-edition manual page break unchanged into the teacher edition. Let added guidance reflow naturally, then inspect the teacher contact sheet for pages that contain only one detached note block.
- Inspect the page immediately before every manual or semantic break. A breakable exercise can spill one final row, option pair, or question onto a fragment page and then meet the forced break. Move the whole task group or shift the break; writing lines do not make a detached fragment acceptable.

## Preserve restraint

- Use the bundled template tokens and components as the base system. Add local subject components on top; do not recreate the base palette, cover, page header, or primitives in a parallel theme.
- Prefer one calm accent and one supporting signal to a full palette.
- Avoid gradients, shadows, stickers, stock imagery, dashboard-like grids, and decorative labels.
- Do not use color as the only carrier of task meaning; headings and wording must remain clear in grayscale.

## Check at reading distance

Ask whether a learner can locate the next action, attach every mark to one A–D label, identify the writing area, and distinguish instruction from content within a few seconds. If not, simplify hierarchy before adding emphasis. For editorial self-study documents, also compare several consecutive pages: the book should feel continuous without becoming monotonous, and explanation, example, caution, practice, and writing pages should not all share one fixed skeleton. Compare the rendered page count with the locked brief before delivery; a near-empty overflow page is a composition failure, not extra breathing room.
