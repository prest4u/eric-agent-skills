# Eric Transaction Dealbook PDF

## Workflow

1. Lock the decision use, audience, exact inputs, page sequence, derivative output path, and no-overwrite policy.
2. Map the material into evidence-bearing roles: thesis, falsifiable diligence questions, dependency assembly, decision gates, responsibility handoff, and explicit unresolved risk.
3. Read [production-grammar.md](references/production-grammar.md) before designing or adapting the PDF.
4. Keep text, rules, and tables as searchable HTML/CSS or another deterministic text layer. Use imagery only as contextual evidence fields, never as page-sized text rasters.
5. Register every image once with source or generation method, prompt or transform, page role, dimensions, no-visible-text policy, and unique use.
6. Build a new derivative; never overwrite the input PDF. Render every page from the final PDF and generate a contact sheet after the final source edit.
7. Verify A4 size, page count, searchability, absent JavaScript, image uniqueness, print contrast, and zero overlap, clipping, masking, overflow, console, or page errors.
8. Inspect the cover, every image-bearing page, the densest page, method/data page, and final ownership page at full size. Freeze PDF, page-render, contact-sheet, source, provenance, and Skill hashes before independent review.

## Boundaries

- Use charcoal as the primary, silver grey as the secondary, and oxblood as the single signal colour unless a different accepted transaction palette is supplied.
- Keep risk, gate, and ownership meaning explicit in labels, numbering, line weight, or position; colour must never carry meaning alone.
- Do not invent transaction facts, valuations, ratios, outcomes, approvals, or legal conclusions.
- Do not reuse one image across pages, generate visible text in imagery, upload private deal material, use paid assets without authority, or publish/install without explicit approval.
- Do not self-approve a frozen final artifact; close the writer window and hand the exact hashes to an independent reviewer.
